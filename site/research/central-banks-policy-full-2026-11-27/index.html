Five key takeaways

1. Fed | Tightening risk: Stronger US growth and persistent inflation reinforce expectations of a December rate increase.

2. UST | Growth versus inflation: Treasury bonds can rally despite hawkish monetary policy when investors become sufficiently concerned about broader market weakness.

4. Asia | Policy normalisation: South Korea has raised rates, while Japan faces increasing costs associated with monetary normalisation.

5. US administration | Tariff uncertainty: Newly proposed tariffs create renewed inflation and trade risks, but their implementation is not immediate.

Beige Book: underlying economic conditions

Economic activity across Fed districts

8 growing

4 unchanged

Reuters, 25 November. Survey information collected through 16 November.

Businesses faced rising input expenses and difficulty passing those expenses to consumers. This creates potential pressure on corporate profitability, even while economic activity remains resilient.

Transmission analysis: Persistent inflation increases the risk of higher short-term rates. Meanwhile, weaker corporate margins could eventually undermine employment, capital expenditure and credit quality.

The resulting environment is not necessarily negative for every bond maturity. Short-end yields are particularly exposed to policy tightening; long-end yields also reflect future growth and recession expectations.
