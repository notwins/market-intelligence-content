04 / Consumer / industrials

Online spending is strong; margins remain untested

Adobe measured $9.7bn online by 6:30pm ET Friday (07:30 SGT Saturday), 12.8% above the same point in 2025. Its full-day forecast is $13bn–$13.4bn, not a final result. NRF projects holiday retail sales growth of 4.2%. A survey found 82% of shoppers taking cost-saving measures. Analysis: monitor discounts, fulfilment costs and gross margins; aggregate online spending is not the revenue of Amazon, Walmart or Target. No fresh industrial earnings pair was located.

05 / Financials / credit

Flows show defensive positioning before the rebound

LSEG Lipper reported equity fund outflows of $30.57bn for the week ended 25 November. Bond funds lost $649m overall. Short-to-intermediate investment-grade funds lost $6.51bn, while short-to-intermediate government/Treasury funds attracted $5.21bn. The flow period ended before Friday’s rally. The figures describe funds, not total securities-market flows or bank earnings. No fresh major listed-bank quarterly pair was verified.

06 / Healthcare / energy

Drug approval timing and fuel costs

Vertex ended Friday down 5.2%; FDA review of povetacicept has been extended to February 28, 2027. Vera Therapeutics rose 14% in a separate morning observation. This is a regulatory delay, not a drug rejection or a quarterly earnings release. Brent settled at $95.44 (−1.2%); WTI at $83.08 (−0.7% versus Wednesday because there was no Thursday settlement). Analysis: fuel costs may pressure transport and retail margins while supporting producer revenue. No new major energy earnings pair was located.

09 / Three client talking points

Separate demand, profitability and financing

1. A price rebound is not a quarterly beat: check actual margins and cash generation. 2. Holiday sales growth is not profit growth: check discount costs and returns. 3. A Treasury allocation is not the same risk as a corporate bond or a CHF-funded USD position: stress-test duration, credit spreads, funding, FX and collateral separately.
