Method and limits

The study screens all 155 saved datasets using the default series, measure and native observation frequency already defined in the FRED catalogue. For each observation, use the previous five calendar years, exclude the evaluated observation, and divide the difference from the prior arithmetic mean by the prior sample standard deviation. At least 12 usable prior observations and a nonzero standard deviation are required. The current latest dated observation determines membership, including unavailable readings. Values and σ are rounded only for display.

Absolute σ determines the tier. Positive and negative scores share each tier. A 1σ or 2σ run begins when a reading meets the threshold after the prior scorable point fell short of it or was on the opposite side of the mean. Missing scores do not establish a crossing. The timeline marks first scorable readings separately and discloses any skipped observations. The report shows the active runs of the 53 current breach readings. Revisions can change the date or the classification.

Market comparisons use first and last eligible saved quotes in the observation month or quarter, or a window of up to ten calendar days either side of a daily or weekly entry. The endpoint never goes beyond the archive date. Yields use basis-point changes. Equity indices and FX use percent changes in native units. No synthetic prices, interpolated points or causal estimates are used. Equity indices are price levels except when the labelled FRED source explicitly supplies a total-return measure.

Latest readings use different publication and observation dates. A five-year window is a maximum and some series start later, including the ICE BofA history in November 2023. Monthly and quarterly chronology is based on observation periods. These revised histories cannot reproduce the information available on each past trading day. A sigma score describes distance from the chosen baseline, and does not estimate probability or investment performance.
