NVIDIA after Jensen Huang
The chips and the checks

Analysis

Two clocks on one ticker

The earnings quarter ended on 25 October. The first public intelligence report followed on 29 October. Those dates put a limit on what Q3 can tell us about the crisis.

The last price on this chart is pre-earnings. NVDA fell from $213.66 on 29 October to $155.46 on 17 November. The results arrived after the closing bell.

A quarter can beat the clock. It cannot turn it back.

Market tape / price and turnover

What the sell-off can measure

The closing tape puts numbers on the crisis. It cannot price the former CEO in isolation. A market-cap loss also differs from cash leaving the company.

Analysis

How much of the fall was market-wide?

NVDA trailed the Nasdaq Composite by 20.15 percentage points. The index lost 7.09% in the same window. The gap measures relative performance. Several news items and changes in market expectations overlap.

By 12 November, NVDA was already down 22.22% from 29 October. That verifies the scale of the decline described in the video on an explicit pair of closing dates.

The index gives the share price a sense of proportion.
Analysis

The tape found its voice

The 12 November close fell 4.34% on 747.7m shares. The move was from $173.73 to $166.19. The Composite fell 0.39% that day.

Zoom out and the baseline changes. NVDA lost 16.69% over the full year from $186.60, and 16.64% from the 31 December close of $186.50.

A sell-off has two sides to every trade.

Earnings report card / reported results

The revenue engine kept accelerating

Q3 crossed $100bn of sales for the first time. The report extends the revenue and demand charts from the 5 November edition. Earlier Nvidia report card.

Earnings gave this quarter more than a quarter turn.
Analysis

A beat against a stated target

Q3 revenue beat management’s $108bn midpoint by $7.84bn. Even the upper end of the prior guide, $110.16bn, was below the result. Sales grew 20% sequentially and 103% year on year.

Diluted EPS reached $2.70, up from $1.30 a year earlier. Operating income reached $77.52bn. Net income was $65.29bn, up 105% year on year.

The top line went over the top of the range.
Analysis

The cloud has a very large bill

Data Center supplied $107.6bn, or 92.9% of sales. The 5 November report card had 92.5% for Q2. Data Center grew 110% year on year, a little faster than Nvidia overall.

The platform mix locates demand. Hyperscale customers alone accounted for more than half of quarterly sales. A change in deployment schedules at large buyers can move the whole quarter.

When hyperscalers scale back, the scales can tip.

Earnings report card / cash quality

How much of the profit became cash?

A sale, a profit and a payment can land in different quarters. Inventory and receivables help explain the distance between the lines.

Analysis

Following the cash receipts

Operating cash flow was $48.25bn, about 74% of net income. Purchases of property, equipment and intangibles used $4.42bn. Related principal payments used $0.05bn, leaving $43.78bn in free cash flow.

Free cash flow more than doubled from Q2’s $21.34bn. Management cited timing of tax payments in the operating cash increase. A single quarter can overstate the change in underlying collection rates.

Profit booked a room. Cash checked in later.
Analysis

When a sale takes longer to collect

Days sales outstanding rose from 60 to 64. Quarter-end receivables reached $81bn. The increase in receivables absorbed $18.05bn of operating cash in Q3.

Two direct customers accounted for 18% and 17% of receivables. Concentration makes payment timing worth tracking alongside sales growth. Longer payment terms can pull the two series apart.

The customers are large. So are their tabs.

Forward view / management guidance

Demand growth comes with commitments

Nvidia expects $130bn of Q4 revenue, plus or minus 3%. The next quarter tests both the Rubin ramp and the cost of reserving supply.

Margins have a thin skin.
Analysis

The margin for error gets smaller

Gross margin was 74.8%, down 0.2 points from Q2. Management attributed the modest sequential decline to initial Rubin production. The Q4 outlook implies another 2.8–3.8 points of compression.

The guide assumes zero China Data Center compute revenue. Q3 included $2.4bn of Hopper Data Center products shipped to China. An extra fiscal week helps the Q4 sales comparison.

A longer quarter buys time by the week.
Analysis

The order book works both ways

Nvidia committed to capacity as well as selling it. Supply and capacity commitments rose from $279bn in July to $390bn in October. Committing ahead can secure scarce inputs, but a demand slowdown would leave less flexibility.

Another $32bn of neocloud agreements depends on conditions. Financing, deployment and acceptance matter. Treat that amount separately from the company’s five-category table.

Capacity has a capacity to surprise.

Financing / risk transmission

Who carries the credit risk?

The balance sheet reaches beyond the chips Nvidia ships. New financing can support demand while tying some of Nvidia’s exposure to the same projects its customers are building.

Analysis

Why a chipmaker is underwriting its ecosystem

A guarantee can help a partner obtain financing. If the partner defaults and contractual conditions are met, the guarantor may have to perform. The SB Energy arrangement phases in as buildings enter service, first expected in FY29.

The $118bn maximum is a different measure from the $518bn commitments table. Nvidia reported no payments under the guarantees to date. A maximum alone gives neither a default probability nor an expected loss.

Underwriting is where the fine print gets a co-signer.
Analysis

Testing the “central bank of AI” joke

Patrick’s analogy describes how financing can help customers buy capacity. A vendor can earn sales today and still bear some customer or project risk later. Signed commitments, maximum guarantees and actual cash payments answer different questions.

The video’s $250bn proposal and October’s $105bn SB Energy cap use different terms. The equity line in the filed table is $20bn. A wider tally of announced deals in the video uses a different perimeter.

The next cash-flow statement gets the last word.

Video timeline / origins

Two investigations, two different questions

The export inquiry predates the leadership crisis. One track follows the movement and use of hardware. The other follows reported intelligence concerns and what the board knew.

A chip can change hands faster than the paperwork.
Evidence and limits

What the trade-record gap means

Megaspeed is a cloud provider with operations in Malaysia. Bloomberg’s 2025 records analysis put its imported Nvidia hardware at more than $4.6bn from 2023 through November 2025. The firm denies unlawful conduct.

The roughly 50,000 chips are a discrepancy between records. The comparison does not locate a shipment. The video also cites 17,544 GB300 chips worth about $787m in a later import record. Neither number alone identifies the eventual end user.

A cloud address tells only part of the storage story.
Reported allegations

What the public record supports

Alice Huang is also identified in reporting as Alice Wong. The intelligence assessment was reported by news organisations. China denied the espionage allegation. An assessed intelligence link is different from a public finding of guilt.

The business relationship and the alleged personal relationship raise separate questions. For shareholders, the verifiable issue is how directors checked what the company could say and how it supervised sensitive customers.

The board needed a fact-check before anyone cashed the assurance.

Video timeline / succession and oversight

Four days changed the boardroom

Huang acknowledged infidelity on 8 November without naming a partner. On 11 November the board accepted his resignation. The public announcement came the next morning.

This was a board meeting with a change of address at the head of the table.
Analysis

The succession question

Colette Kress took the interim role and retained the CFO job. The board began a search covering internal and external candidates. Harvey Jones, a director since 1993, became chair.

The transition filing gives the timing and appointments. The independent board investigation continued. The video questions succession planning, but the filing cannot establish what confidential plans existed.

Two executive hats, one earnings call.
Analysis

A rule and a new committee

BIS announced its interim final rule on remote access on 16 November. The rule addresses third-country computing arrangements. Physical shipment and remote use are two ways restrictions can matter to a cloud operator.

Nvidia formed a standing national security and export-control oversight committee on 17 November. The next evidence will be what the committee reports, how the company changes its controls, and what investigators ultimately find.

A standing committee has work to do after it takes a seat.
