Core research · four original publications

1. BIS Working Paper 1400 | 27 November | Catherine Casanova, Eugenio Cerutti and Swapan-Kumar Pradhan. Cross-border bank claims on non-bank financial institutions (NBFIs) more than doubled over the last decade, while those on the non-financial sector rose approximately 70% and interbank claims excluding related offices about 55%. Since 2022, NBFI claims have been comparable in level to non-financial-sector claims.

BIS paper published 27 November 2026Approximate cumulative growth (%)Cross-border claims growth over the past decadeEstimated growth was greatest for lending to non-bank financial institutions.Read each labelled observation in its stated units.Approximate comparisons from the authors’ reported figures.Source

Method: international banking statistics across 30 banking nationalities, 38 reporting locations and 180 counterparty countries; excluding home-country claims. Graph 1 and cover visually checked. Regression evidence concerns booking geography, not causation or the ultimate borrower’s probability of default. Holdings are not necessarily newly originated loans. Positioning: monitor who provides financing to funds, the jurisdiction where it is booked, and who bears liquidity risk. Client point: the bank may still finance the lender even when it no longer directly finances the company.

Sources: BIS Working Paper 1400: Banking across borders: where to lend from differs by sector

2. Federal Reserve commercial paper update | 27 November | data through 25 November. Outstanding US commercial paper maturing after 31 December increased from $701.5bn on 18 November to $759.5bn on 25 November. The corresponding shares rose from 48.9% to 53.2%; asset-backed CP’s share rose from 56.0% to 61.2%.

The Fed derives rates from DTCC trade data. On 25 November, 30-day AA nonfinancial CP was 4.12% and A2/P2 nonfinancial CP 4.30%, a difference of 18bp. Total seasonally adjusted CP outstanding fell $26.6bn during the week, led by a $21.7bn decline in financial CP. These are stock changes, not gross new borrowing or proof of defaults. Positioning: maturities beyond year-end can reduce immediate rollover needs, but investors should track the subsequent refinancing calendar. Client point: spreading repayments buys time; it does not reduce the amount owed.

3. ECB October monetary developments | 26 November | official statistical release. Adjusted corporate-loan growth accelerated to 4.5% from revised 4.3%, household-loan growth remained 3.1%, and M3 grew 3.6% in both months. M1 growth fell from 2.7% to 2.4%.

The speech distinguishes stablecoins from tokenised bank deposits and discusses how new settlement systems can preserve parity and legal finality. The rendered PDF cover and p.2 were inspected; pp.4–5 discuss cyber resilience and enforceability. It is a policy speech, not a quantitative estimate of lower credit losses.

Interactive financing stress · synthetic example

Assumptions: USD1,000,000 starting gross assets, USD700,000 loan, modified duration 16, convexity 300, maximum LTV 75%. Rate and spread sensitivities are assumed equal for illustration. Price change = −16Δy + 150Δy², where Δy = (rate shock + spread shock)/10,000. Gross assets = initial assets × (1 + change); translated loan = original loan × (1 + FX change/100); net equity = assets − loan; headroom = LTV × assets − loan. No coupon, defaults, fees, hedging or actual bank-specific rules included.
