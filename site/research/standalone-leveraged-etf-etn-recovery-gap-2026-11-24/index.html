01 / The evidence

A rebound starts from what is left.

The important number is remaining capital. In the selected 6 October–20 November 2026 screenshot interval, SOXX fell 37.20% while SOXL fell 81.20%. Normalize each investment to $100 and the consequence becomes clear: $62.80 versus $18.80 remains.

What the screenshots show

Even the short window differs sharply. The supplied past-week display shows SOXX at −0.66% and SOXL at −11.15%. Three times −0.66% is −1.98%; the observed display is 9.17 percentage points below that simple comparison.

The three-month display tells the same cautionary story. SOXX is −16.90% and SOXL −61.13%. Three times −16.90% is −50.70%, leaving a 10.43 percentage-point gap against that naive multiple.

What the screenshots cannot prove

Those gaps are not isolated measurements of “decay”. A proper attribution needs aligned daily benchmark returns, adjusted product returns, fees, financing and tracking data. These screenshots do not provide that series.

Use matching windows. The SOXX +13.07% selected rebound begins at an intraday point. There is no matching SOXL selection here, so it cannot support a claim that SOXL gained exactly +39.21%. The app’s latest headline price is also not necessarily the selected interval’s endpoint.

02 / The mechanism

The term is volatility drag.

“Volatility decay” is common shorthand; daily-reset compounding and path dependence explain it more precisely. SOXL seeks 300% of its benchmark’s daily return before fees and expenses. SOXX is an unleveraged comparator. Both reference the NYSE Semiconductor Index, but SOXL’s objective is tied to the index, not to SOXX’s traded share price. Direxion objective · iShares benchmark

The daily arithmetic

Each new day starts from a new capital base. If the benchmark’s daily return is r, the ideal 3× day multiplies the investment by (1 + 3r). Over several days those factors multiply together.

1× cumulative return = ∏ (1 + rt) − 1
Daily 3× cumulative return = ∏ (1 + 3rt) − 1

Ideal model before costs and tracking differences, with valid positive daily factors. A period’s benchmark return alone does not determine the daily 3× outcome.

The exposure reset

Start with $100 supporting $300 of exposure. A 20% benchmark fall loses $60, leaving $40. To restore 3× leverage, the next day’s exposure is $120.

A 25% rebound earns $30 on that $120 exposure. Capital becomes $70. The benchmark has recovered from 80 to 100, yet the leveraged holding remains 30% below its starting capital.

This simplified exposure calculation excludes financing, transaction costs and any exceptional rebalancing provisions. It explains the hypothetical model; it is not a reconstruction of the actual fund’s trades.

Time alone does not impose a fixed daily percentage loss. A genuinely flat daily benchmark leaves both ideal products flat before costs. Repeated reversals can erode capital, while persistent gains can compound favourably. The SEC explains why holding-period outcomes can depart substantially from the stated daily multiple. 03 / The scenario lab

Same starting amount. Different paths.

A market view must include the path and holding period. These six deliberately simple experiments isolate daily compounding. They show favourable and unfavourable outcomes without presenting a forecast.

What “path dependence” means here

Two paths can end at the same benchmark level and produce different leveraged results. Flat, flat leaves the daily 3× model at $100; −20%, +25% leaves it at $70; repeating that round trip leaves it at $49. Simply rearranging the same set of daily returns would not change the product in this ideal model, because multiplication is commutative.

The recovery percentage belongs to the fund itself. The index gain that would restore SOXL depends on the subsequent daily path and costs. Dividing 431.914893617021…% by three does not produce a reliable multi-day index target.

A useful inversion

Ask what must happen to get back to your starting capital. A low share price relative to an old high does not establish cheapness. A reverse split can change the quoted share price and share count without repairing the investor’s economic loss.

05 / The product structure

ETF or ETN? First identify what you own.

SOXL is a leveraged ETF; it is not an ETN. An ETF’s structure and its leverage objective answer different questions. The same distinction applies to YINN. Unleveraged funds can still be risky, and unleveraged notes still carry an issuer-credit claim.

01

Daily leverage

Large losses shrink the base for the rebound. SOXL, YINN and daily leveraged notes can deviate sharply from a simple holding-period multiple. SOXL’s prospectus warns that an investor can lose all principal in a single trading day. Inverse notes also reset; −3× daily does not promise minus three times a quarter’s return.

02

Financing & implementation

The expense ratio is not the whole economic cost. Derivative financing, transaction costs, spreads and imperfect tracking can affect realized returns. Keep these effects separate from the ideal compounding examples. SOXL prospectus

03

Issuer credit & calls

An ETN is a bank obligation. Repayment depends on the issuer and contractual terms; issuer failure can cause loss of the entire investment. A call can end the position on the issuer’s timetable; the exchange price can also depart from indicative value. SEC ETN bulletin

04

Futures roll

VXX and VXZ track VIX futures, not spot VIX. They are unleveraged ETNs. The futures curve and rolling contracts can erode returns even without a 3× reset. The effect depends on the curve and is distinct from daily leverage drag. Barclays pricing supplement

05

Income strategy trade-offs

A distribution is not a guaranteed profit. Covered-call notes such as GLDI, SLVO and USOI combine underlying exposure with option writing. Evaluate total return, downside and foregone upside; option time decay is a separate mechanism from leveraged compounding. UBS: USOI terms

06

Concentration & currency

A diversified wrapper can still hold a narrow exposure. Semiconductor, China, oil and FANG+ products concentrate risk differently. For a Singapore-dollar investor, USD investment performance and USD/SGD conversion are separate contributors to the final SGD result.

A current example · 12 November 2026

Catalogue presence does not mean an open-ended investment horizon.

UBS announced the redemption of ten ETNs, with settlements scheduled for 17–18 December 2026. BDCX, BDCZ, CEFD, HDLB, IFED, MLPR, MVRL, PFFL, SMHB and UCIB are excluded from the 20-note directory below. They may still appear on a product catalogue before the redemption completes. UBS announcement

06 / The China comparison

YINN applies the same daily-reset lesson.

FXI · unleveraged ETF

A China large-cap exposure

FXI tracks the FTSE China 50 Index. It provides an unleveraged comparator for the benchmark underlying YINN. It is not a complete representation of every Chinese equity market or a promise of capital stability. iShares FXI

YINN · daily +3× ETF

A leveraged daily trade

YINN targets +300% of the FTSE China 50 Index’s daily return before costs. A bullish long-term view on China does not by itself establish that YINN is the right holding vehicle. Daily reversals can change the result materially. Direxion YINN

The scenario mathematics transfers; the historical numbers do not. A hypothetical −20%, +25% benchmark round trip would produce the same $100-to-$70 ideal 3× result. No YINN or FXI historical return is assumed or inferred from the semiconductor screenshots. China policy, constituent concentration and currency exposures remain additional risks.

07 / The reference directory

40 products. Two legal structures.

Start with identity, exposure and reset terms before looking at a chart. These are verified examples with primary-source references, not a buy list or a live price sheet. The ETF selection includes every one of the 11 US Select Sector SPDR sectors.

Three ticker traps

QQQ is not a pure technology-sector fund. Its Nasdaq-100 exposure spans non-financial companies across sectors. XLK is the dedicated information-technology sector entry in this directory.

NRGU and OILU reference energy-company shares. Their economic exposure differs from crude oil futures. USOI, in turn, is a covered-call strategy linked to USO shares; its name should not be read as direct spot-oil ownership.

Two series traps

Current FNGU is the 2045 series formerly called FNGB. The ticker changed on 24 June 2025. Do not splice older FNGU history onto the current note without checking the exact series and adjustments.

GLDI, SLVO and USOI are now UBS AG obligations. Their former Credit Suisse association does not identify the current legal issuer. The linked terms are the relevant starting point.

08 / My thoughts

Choose the vehicle for the journey.

My view: a long-term sector thesis and a daily leveraged trade require different disciplines. The semiconductor screenshots illustrate how quickly the recovery hurdle can become the dominant portfolio problem. “The sector will eventually recover” is an incomplete investment case for a daily 3× holding.

For sustained exposure, start by comparing unleveraged vehicles and concentration. SOXX and FXI offer simpler benchmark exposure than SOXL and YINN respectively, although both can still suffer substantial losses. SPY, QQQ and the sector funds solve different portfolio objectives; overlap should be assessed at the underlying holdings level.

For a leveraged position, define the trade before entry. Specify the catalyst, intended horizon, position size, maximum tolerable dollar loss, review frequency and conditions that invalidate the thesis. Stops can help discipline execution, but gaps and fast markets can cause fills beyond the intended level.

For an ETN, add a credit-and-contract review. Identify the legal issuer and exact series, compare market price with indicative value, check spreads, calls and redemption provisions, and confirm that the product remains available through the intended broker.

BEFORE BUYING

Write the exposure sentence.

“I am buying [fund or bank note], linked to [benchmark], with [multiplier] resetting [period], for [intended horizon].” If that sentence is unclear, the investment case is incomplete.

WHILE HOLDING

Track dollars and the thesis.

Monitor remaining capital, exposure, liquidity and whether the original reason still holds. A lower average cost does not change the product’s daily reset or eliminate further downside.

WHEN REVIEWING

Calculate the recovery hurdle.

Use loss ÷ remaining value. Then ask whether the return path and risk required to recover are still acceptable. The original purchase price is a reference point, not a valuation model.

The recovery of an index and the recovery of a leveraged holding are different events.

09 / Method & limitations

What is observed, calculated and assumed.

Observed inputs: the supplied Apple Stocks / Yahoo Finance screenshots display SOXX/SOXL returns of −0.66%/−11.15% over “Past Week”, −16.90%/−61.13% over “Past 3 Months”, and −37.20%/−81.20% over the selected 6 October–20 November 2026 interval. These figures are attributed to the supplied images; they have not been reconstructed from an independent adjusted daily return series. Original screenshots and private conversation material are not republished.

Calculated outputs: remaining capital equals starting capital × (1 + displayed return). Loss equals starting capital minus remaining capital. Recovery equals loss ÷ remaining capital × 100%. Every static financial calculation uses rational arithmetic. Finite decimals are exact; repeating decimals show the first 12 decimal digits followed by an ellipsis and an exact fraction where needed. The displayed source percentages themselves may already be rounded.

Hypothetical assumptions: each path starts at USD 100, with perfect daily 1× or 3× exposure, no cash flows, fees, financing, distributions, taxes or tracking error. These scenarios are explanatory experiments. They are not realized fund data, expected returns or a probability model. The visual geometry is illustrative; exact values are in the tables.

Product verification: issuer product pages, pricing supplements, SEC filings and exchange listings were reviewed at the research cutoff. Source documents can have earlier effective dates. The directory records identities and stated objectives, not executable prices, live liquidity, new-issuance availability or investor eligibility. Redemption notices identified in the review were considered; later announcements may change the list.

Interpretation: price-return gaps cannot be attributed solely to volatility drag without an aligned daily analysis. ETF expenses, derivative financing, market-price/NAV differences, distributions and measurement windows can also matter. Return examples are in USD; no SGD exchange-rate assumption is embedded. This is personal educational research, not an institution’s research publication or an individualized investment recommendation.
