What changed and why it matters

Policy: a cross-market rate event, not only a Fed event

December central-bank risk is now synchronised. That raises the probability of correlated front-end repricing, even if the long end is supported by risk aversion. Treasury duration can hedge a growth shock but remains vulnerable to inflation and terminal-rate surprises.

Tariffs: caps, conditions and timing are now clearer

The 25 November notice proposes a maximum of 20% for covered Singapore goods, net of existing Section 301 tariffs. USTR may consider reducing it to 10% depending on Singapore’s commitments. Other proposals include a 20% China rate while the truce holds and 15% proposals for Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, the EU and Switzerland, subject to terms and conditions. The proposal cannot take effect before a 25 January 2027 hearing. This is an implementation risk for inflation, trade and supply chains—not an immediate realised cash-flow event.

The 25 November proposal’s maximum and its conditional lower option have separate bases. USTR notice for Singapore

FX: carry is not a complete hedge

In a risk shock, USD can benefit from US rates while CHF and JPY benefit from safe-haven flows. CHF-funded USD portfolios should stress both asset prices and FX; Treasury gains do not necessarily offset CHF appreciation.
